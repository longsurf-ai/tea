Volume-weighted moving average: the mean of the last `length` values of `source`, each weighted by its bar's [`volume`](../price-and-volume.md#volume).

```tea
ta.vwma(float source, int length)
```

| Parameter | Type    | Description                                                         |
| --------- | ------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `source`  | `float` | Series to average, usually [`close`](../price-and-volume.md#close). |
| `length`  | `int`   | Number of bars in the window, at least 1.                           |

**Returns:** `float`: the weighted mean of the window.

It is the sum of `source * volume` divided by the sum of `volume`, over the
bars in the window where neither is `na`.

**Formula**

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{vwma}_t &= \frac
{\sum_{i \in K_t} \mathit{source}_{t-i} \cdot \mathit{volume}_{t-i}}
{\sum_{i \in K_t} \mathit{volume}_{t-i}} \\
\text{where}\quad
K_t &= \{\, i : 0 \le i < \mathit{length},
\ \mathit{source}_{t-i} \ne \text{na},
\ \mathit{volume}_{t-i} \ne \text{na} \,\}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Warm-up and na:** `na` on the first `length - 1` bars, when no bar in the window has
both `source` and `volume`, and when the volumes of the bars used add up
to 0.

**Example:** A 3-bar average. At index 3 it is
`(12 * 300 + 11 * 100 + 14 * 200) / 600 = 12.5`. The close at index 4 is
missing, so that bar's volume is left out too: at index 4 the result is
`(11 * 100 + 14 * 200) / 300 = 13`.

```tea
emit "close" close
emit "volume" volume
emit "vwma" ta.vwma(close, 3)
```

```csv
time,close,volume
0,10,100
1,12,300
2,11,100
3,14,200
4,,300
5,16,200
6,19,100
7,21,100
```

**Output:**

```text
index  close  volume  vwma
0      10     100     na
1      12     300     na
2      11     100     11.4
3      14     200     12.5
4      na     300     13
5      16     200     15
6      19     100     17
7      21     100     18
```

**Pine Script:** Pine's manual defines it as
`ta.sma(source * volume, length) / ta.sma(volume, length)`. The two differ
when `source` is `na` on a bar whose volume is known: Tea leaves that
volume out, while `ta.sma(volume, length)` still counts it.
