Parabolic SAR (stop and reverse): a trailing stop level that follows the trend and speeds up as the trend makes new extremes.

```tea
ta.sar(simple float start, simple float inc, simple float max)
```

| Parameter | Type           | Description                                                                                                                                             |
| --------- | -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `start`   | `simple float` | Initial acceleration factor, such as `0.02`. It must be known when the script starts, such as a constant or an input.                                   |
| `inc`     | `simple float` | Increase of the acceleration factor at each new extreme point, such as `0.02`. It must be known when the script starts, such as a constant or an input. |
| `max`     | `simple float` | Largest acceleration factor, such as `0.2`. It must be known when the script starts, such as a constant or an input.                                    |

**Returns:** `float`: the stop level.

A bar counts when the call runs on it and its [`high`](../price-and-volume.md#high), [`low`](../price-and-volume.md#low) and
[`close`](../price-and-volume.md#close) are all known. On a bar with a missing price the level holds.
Below, "the previous bar" means the latest earlier bar that counts.

The trend starts on the second bar that counts. If the close rose from the
previous bar, it is an uptrend: the level starts at the previous bar's low
and the extreme point at this bar's high. Otherwise it is a downtrend: the
level starts at the previous bar's high and the extreme point at this bar's
low. The acceleration factor starts at `start`. Then each bar that counts,
from that one on, takes these steps:

1. The level moves toward the extreme point by the acceleration factor
   times the distance between them.
2. In an uptrend, if that level is above the bar's low, the trend turns
   down: the level jumps to the higher of the extreme point and the bar's
   high, the extreme point becomes the bar's low, and the factor returns to
   `start`. A downtrend turns up the same way when the level is below the
   bar's high.
3. Otherwise, a high above the extreme point in an uptrend, or a low below
   it in a downtrend, becomes the new extreme point and raises the factor by
   `inc`, up to `max`.
4. If the trend is now up, the level is lowered to the lowest low of the
   previous two bars if it is above it; if it is now down, the level is
   raised to their highest high if it is below it. On the bar where the
   trend starts, there is only one previous bar.

Step 2 compares the bar with the level from step 1, before step 4.

**Formula**

$$
\begin{aligned}
S^{*}_t &= S_p + a_p \cdot (E_p - S_p) \\[4pt]
&\text{in an uptrend:} \\
S_t &= \begin{cases}
  \max(E_p, \mathit{high}_t, \mathit{high}_p, \mathit{high}_q)
    & S^{*}_t > \mathit{low}_t \text{ (turns down)} \\
  \min(S^{*}_t, \mathit{low}_p, \mathit{low}_q) & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases} \\
(E_t, a_t) &= \begin{cases}
  (\mathit{low}_t, \mathit{start}) & S^{*}_t > \mathit{low}_t \\
  (\mathit{high}_t, \min(a_p + \mathit{inc}, \mathit{max}))
    & \mathit{high}_t > E_p \\
  (E_p, a_p) & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases} \\[4pt]
&\text{in a downtrend:} \\
S_t &= \begin{cases}
  \min(E_p, \mathit{low}_t, \mathit{low}_p, \mathit{low}_q)
    & S^{*}_t < \mathit{high}_t \text{ (turns up)} \\
  \max(S^{*}_t, \mathit{high}_p, \mathit{high}_q) & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases} \\
(E_t, a_t) &= \begin{cases}
  (\mathit{high}_t, \mathit{start}) & S^{*}_t < \mathit{high}_t \\
  (\mathit{low}_t, \min(a_p + \mathit{inc}, \mathit{max}))
    & \mathit{low}_t < E_p \\
  (E_p, a_p) & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases} \\[4pt]
&\text{where the trend starts, without the terms for bar } q\text{:} \\
(S_p, E_p, a_p) &= \begin{cases}
  (\mathit{low}_p, \mathit{high}_t, \mathit{start}) \text{, uptrend}
    & \mathit{close}_t > \mathit{close}_p \\
  (\mathit{high}_p, \mathit{low}_t, \mathit{start}) \text{, downtrend}
    & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases} \\[4pt]
&\text{where } p, q \text{ are the latest and second-latest earlier bars
  that count,} \\
&\text{and } S_t = S_{t-1} \text{ on a bar with a missing high, low or close}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Warm-up and na:** `na` until the second bar on which the call runs with a known high,
low and close: only on the first bar for a call that runs on every bar with
no missing price. After that it is never `na`: a bar with a missing high,
low or close keeps the previous level. An `na` `start`, `inc` or `max`
gives `na` on every bar.

**Example:** The close rises at index 1, so the trend starts up from the previous low,
8; the level then climbs, holding at 8.8 at index 4, where the low is
missing. At index 6 the low of 9 is below the level, so the trend turns
down and the level jumps to 14, the highest high of the uptrend.

```tea
emit "high" high
emit "low" low
emit "close" close
emit "sar" ta.sar(0.1, 0.1, 0.2)
```

**Pine Script:** The reference code in Pine's manual starts the trend on the bar where
`bar_index` is `1` and does not skip bars with a missing price. Tea starts
on the second bar on which the call runs with a known high, low and close,
and skips the bars without them; when every price is known and the call
runs on every bar, the levels are the same.
