Bollinger Bands: the simple moving average of `series`, with bands `mult` standard deviations above and below it.

```tea
ta.bb(float series, int length, simple float mult)
```

| Parameter | Type           | Description                                                                                                                 |
| --------- | -------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `series`  | `float`        | Series to measure, usually [`close`](../price-and-volume.md#close).                                                         |
| `length`  | `int`          | Number of bars in the window, at least 1.                                                                                   |
| `mult`    | `simple float` | Band distance in standard deviations, such as `2`. It must be known when the script starts, such as a constant or an input. |

**Returns:** `[float, float, float]`: the middle band, the upper band and the lower band.

The middle band is [`ta.sma`](./sma.md) and the distance is `mult` times the
population standard deviation, [`ta.stdev`](./stdev.md) with `biased` `true`, both
over the last `length` values. [`ta.bbw`](./bbw.md) gives the distance between
the bands relative to the middle band.

**Formula**

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathit{middle}_t &= \mathrm{sma}(\mathit{series}, \mathit{length})_t \\
\mathit{upper}_t &= \mathit{middle}_t
  + \mathit{mult} \cdot \mathrm{stdev}(\mathit{series}, \mathit{length})_t \\
\mathit{lower}_t &= \mathit{middle}_t
  - \mathit{mult} \cdot \mathrm{stdev}(\mathit{series}, \mathit{length})_t
\end{aligned}
$$

**Warm-up and na:** All three are `na` on the first `length - 1` bars, and when every
value in the window is `na`. Other `na` values are left out of the window.

**Example:** At index 3 the window holds 10, 12, 10 and 12: the middle band is 11 and
the standard deviation 1, so the bands are 13 and 9. The close is missing
at index 4, so the windows that hold it use their other three values.

```tea
[middle, upper, lower] = ta.bb(close, 4, 2)
emit "close" close
emit "middle" middle
emit "upper" upper
emit "lower" lower
```
