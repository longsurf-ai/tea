Arnaud Legoux moving average: the mean of the last `length` values of `series`, weighted by a bell curve.

```tea
ta.alma(
    float series,
    int length,
    simple float offset,
    simple float sigma,
    simple bool floor = false
)
```

| Parameter | Type           | Default | Description                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| --------- | -------------- | ------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `series`  | `float`        |         | Series to average, usually [`close`](../price-and-volume.md#close).                                                                                                                                          |
| `length`  | `int`          |         | Number of bars in the window, at least 1.                                                                                                                                                                    |
| `offset`  | `simple float` |         | Position of the peak weight, from 0 (the oldest value) to 1 (the newest). It must be known when the script starts, such as a constant or an input.                                                           |
| `sigma`   | `simple float` |         | Sharpness of the curve: its standard deviation is `length / sigma` bars, so a larger `sigma` puts more of the weight near the peak. It must be known when the script starts, such as a constant or an input. |
| `floor`   | `simple bool`  | `false` | `true` rounds the peak position down to a whole bar; `false`, the default, keeps it as computed. It must be known when the script starts, such as a constant or an input.                                    |

**Returns:** `float`: the weighted mean of the window.

The weights follow a Gaussian curve across the window. Its peak sits
`offset * (length - 1)` positions after the oldest value, and its standard
deviation is `length / sigma` bars. The weighted sum is divided by the sum
of the weights. An `na` value in the window makes the result `na`.

**Formula**

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{alma}_t &= \frac
{\sum_{j=0}^{\mathit{length}-1} w_j
\cdot \mathit{series}_{t-\mathit{length}+1+j}}
{\sum_{j=0}^{\mathit{length}-1} w_j},
\qquad w_j = \exp\left(-\frac{(j - m)^2}{2 s^2}\right) \\
\text{where}\quad m &= \begin{cases}
\lfloor \mathit{offset} \cdot (\mathit{length} - 1) \rfloor
& \text{if } \mathit{floor} \\
\mathit{offset} \cdot (\mathit{length} - 1) & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases},
\qquad s = \frac{\mathit{length}}{\mathit{sigma}}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Warm-up and na:** `na` on the first `length - 1` bars, and while any value in the
window is `na`.

**Example:** Over 4 bars with `offset` 0.85, the peak sits at position 2.55, between the
two newest closes. `floor = true` moves it back to position 2, so older
closes weigh more; as these closes trend up, the floored average is lower
on every bar that has a value. The close at index 6 is missing, so indexes
6 to 9 are `na`.

```tea
emit "close" close
emit "alma" ta.alma(close, 4, 0.85, 6)
emit "floored" ta.alma(close, 4, 0.85, 6, true)
```
