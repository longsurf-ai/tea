Range of the last `length` values of `source`: the highest minus the lowest.

```tea
ta.range(float source, int length)
```

| Parameter | Type    | Description                                                         |
| --------- | ------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `source`  | `float` | Series to measure, such as [`close`](../price-and-volume.md#close). |
| `length`  | `int`   | Number of bars in the window, at least 1.                           |

**Returns:** `float`: the highest value in the window minus the lowest.

It is [`ta.highest`](./highest.md) minus [`ta.lowest`](./lowest.md) with the same arguments.
Values that are `na` are left out of both.

**Formula**

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{range}_t &= \max_{i \in K_t} \mathit{source}_{t-i}
- \min_{i \in K_t} \mathit{source}_{t-i} \\
K_t &= \{\, i : 0 \le i < \mathit{length},
\ \mathit{source}_{t-i} \ne \text{na} \,\}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Warm-up and na:** `na` on the first `length - 1` bars, and when every value in the
window is `na`.

**Example:** At index 3 the closes 12, 11 and 15 give 15 minus 11, which is 4. The
windows that hold the missing close at index 4 use their two known values.

```tea
emit "close" close
emit "range" ta.range(close, 3)
```

```csv
time,close
0,10
1,12
2,11
3,15
4,
5,13
6,9
7,10
```

**Output:**

```text
index  close  range
0      10     na
1      12     na
2      11     2
3      15     4
4      na     4
5      13     2
6      9      4
7      10     4
```

**Pine Script:** Pine's manual gives an `int` result for an `int` source; Tea's
result is always `float`.

**See also:** [`ta.highest`](./highest.md), [`ta.lowest`](./lowest.md)
